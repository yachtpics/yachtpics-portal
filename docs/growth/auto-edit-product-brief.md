# Auto Edit as a product — brief for the Auto Edit session

*From the portal session, September 12, 2026. Paste this into the chat that built Auto Edit ("Lightroom performance issue Auto Edit") so it starts with what we concluded here.*

## Charlie's idea, in his words

> One-time fee to purchase something like Auto Edit would be worth it just because of the volume we do. Instant savings and more control. Even small-volume studios — pay once, use forever, and it constantly learns your style as you go. It doesn't do all the bells and whistles, but that's the simplicity of it, and for the price it's still a huge savings, you own it, and you don't have to upload to cloud services.

Saved about $3,000 a year across Charlie and Samantha versus Imagen.

## What the market looks like right now (checked Sept 12, 2026)

The category has moved from per-photo to flat subscriptions. Aftershoot Edit $30/mo, Narrative $20–60, FilterPixel $15–67, Neurapix $50 unlimited, Imagen Limitless $129–165/mo (per-photo 5¢ survives only as an on-ramp). Evoto is credits-only. Everything runs in the cloud. Nobody sells a perpetual licence for a style-learning editor.

Aftershoot and Narrative now run style marketplaces paying creators 100% of sales; Neurapix lets photographers price their own presets (€189–330); Imagen's Talent shop is invite-only. A "YachtPics — Marine" style on those platforms is a separate, zero-build revenue idea worth keeping in view.

## The position

**Pay once. Runs on your machine. Learns you as you go.**

That's the one position the subscription tools can't take without breaking their own business model. Affinity did this against Adobe. The customers: studios shooting volume in a consistent style — real estate, marine, automotive, events, school/sports — who resent $360–2,000 a year and don't love uploading client work to someone's servers. "Simple" is a feature to them.

## Honest caveats

- The money is in the tail: a $249 licence needs ~1,000 customers for $250k. It's a real small product business, not a flagship.
- The work isn't the model, it's the product around it: installer, licence keys, Lightroom-version support, the "it doesn't work on my catalog" emails, tutorial videos, a support address that isn't Charlie's phone.
- The 80-minute retrain and "writes into Lightroom" are the two things a stranger will trip on. They need to be bulletproof and explained in one screen.

## Validate before packaging (one afternoon, no code)

A one-page site: three before/afters, the one-line position, a founder price, an email field. Post in five photographer Facebook groups: "I built this because I got tired of paying to edit in my own style." Fifty emails in a week is a business; five is a hobby.

## Questions for the Auto Edit session

1. What does a stranger need on their machine to run it (Python? Lightroom version? Windows only?) — and can it become a single installer?
2. How is the model stored per user, and can retraining be made incremental so "learns as you go" is true without an 80-minute wait each time?
3. Licence mechanism: simplest honest option (offline key, no phone-home) that still lets you cut someone off.
4. What breaks if two photographers on one machine (Charlie + Samantha) each have a profile — that's the studio case.
5. Suggested price: $249 perpetual, $49/yr optional updates; or $199/$29. Argue for one.
